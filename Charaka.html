Sushruta
  • ​Charaka Samhita: The most authoritative text on Internal Medicine (Kayachikitsa).

  • ​Root Cause Analysis: He emphasized that a physician must investigate the cause of a disease rather than just treating the symptoms.

  • ​Metabolism and Digestion: He was the first to describe the three stages of digestion (Madhyama, Prapaka, etc.) and their effect on the body.

  • ​Circulation: He understood the heart as a pump connected to the entire body through various channels.

  • ​Genetics: He postulated that the gender and traits of a child are determined by the dominance of "Beeja" (genetic material) from parents.

  • ​Immunity (Vyadhikshamatva): He introduced the concept that the body has a natural ability to resist and fight disease.

  • ​Pathology: He categorized diseases into those caused by exogenous (external) and endogenous (internal) factors.

  • ​Pharmacopeia: He listed over 500 drugs derived from plants, animals, and minerals.

  • ​Detoxification: He refined the procedures of Panchakarma for restoring metabolic balance.

  • ​Mental Health: He recognized "Unmada" (mental disorders) and suggested spiritual, physical, and medicinal therapies for them.

  • ​Preventive Care: He famously stated, "Prevention is better than cure," focusing on lifestyle as the primary medicine.

  • ​Standardized Dosage: He provided specific instructions on how to prepare decoctions, powders, and pastes for maximum efficacy.

  • ​The Physician’s Oath: He prescribed a rigorous ethical code for doctors, including maintaining patient confidentiality.

  • ​Diagnosis (Eight-fold): He standardized the examination of pulse, urine, stool, tongue, voice, touch, sight, and appearance.

  • ​Concept of Taste: He explained how the six tastes affect the internal chemistry and hormonal balance.

  • ​Environment and Health: He noted how climate, seasons, and local water quality impact the prevalence of specific diseases.

  • ​Geriatrics: He developed therapies specifically for the elderly to maintain vitality and memory.

  • ​Synergy: He believed that the doctor, the patient, the nurse, and the medicine are the four pillars of successful treatment.

  • ​Tissue Vitality: He focused on the "Dhatus" (tissues) and how nutrients are converted into energy.

  • ​Logic in Medicine: He used "Anumana" (inference) and "Pratyaksha" (direct observation) to reach a diagnosis.

  • ​Longevity: His work is titled "Science of Life" because he aimed to help humans live 100 years in peak health.
