Rani ki Vav
  • Rani ki Vav is located on the banks of the Saraswati River in the cityof Patan,Gujarat

  • Built by Queen Udayamati in the 11th century, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Located about 130 km from Ahmedabad, this stepwell is renowned for its architectural excellence

  • The best time to visit Rani ki Vav (Patan, Gujarat) is fromOctober to February (winter months) , when the weather is pleasant and ideal for sightseeing.

  • It features a 64-meter-long, 27-meter-deep, and 20-meter-wide corridor, designed with seven levels of stairs, four pavilions, and over 500 principal sculptures, primarily celebrating Vishnu.

  • It was built in 11th century by Queen Udayamati of the Solanki dynasty in memory of her husband, King Bhimdev I.

  • It is believed that this stepwell was buried under silt due to a massive flood in the Saraswati River in the 13th century.

  • After being buried in the soil for about 700 years, it was rediscovered and cleared by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) in the 1940s.

  • In 2018, the Reserve Bank of India printed an image of Rani ki Vav on the reverse of the ₹100 note, depicting its historical grandeur.

  • Its walls have more than 500 main sculptures and over 1,000 miniature carvings, mainly depicting the incarnations of Lord Vishnu, apsaras and mythological stories.

  • The central theme of the sculptures is the Dashavatara, or the ten incarnations of Lord Vishnu.

  • There is a 30-kilometre-long secret tunnel (now blocked) at the lowest level that once led to the town of Sidhpur, used by the royal family as an escape route during wars

    • .
  • The "Cleanest" Monument: In 2016, it was awarded the title of "Cleanest Iconic Place" in India at the Indian Sanitation Conference.

  • It is considered one of the finest examples of the Maru-Gurjara architectural style, showcasing complex techniques and beautiful proportions.

  • Historically, the water in the stepwell was believed to have medicinal properties due to the herbs that grew around it and the minerals in the silt.

  • It served as a sophisticated groundwater management system, collecting and storing water for the arid region.

  • It was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage site in June 2014 for its cultural and architectural significance.

  • It uses advanced technology to utilize groundwater resources, which is a landmark example of water conservation.

  • Designed as an inverted temple, it highlights the sacredness of water by descending into the earth rather than rising to the sky.

  • The design includes more than 200 pillars and numerous ornate beams.

  • Many carvings depict Patola saree designs, reflecting the local weaving traditions of Patan.

  • Because it was buried in silt, most of the intricate carvings were found in excellent condition.

  • Every surface is adorned with images of apsaras (celestial dancers), surasundaris (mythical beauties), and various deities.
