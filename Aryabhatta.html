Aryabhat
  • Aryabhatiya: His magnum opus, written at age 23, covers arithmetic, algebra, plane trigonometry, and spherical trigonometry.

  • Place Value System: He utilized a place-value system, which is the bedrock of modern digital computing and mathematics.

  • The Value of pi: He calculated the value of pi as 3.1416, describing it as an "approximate" value—noting its irrational nature centuries before Western math.

  • Earth’s Rotation: He was the first to state that the Earth rotates on its axis, causing day and night, rather than the sky moving around the Earth.

  • Solar and Lunar Eclipses: He provided a scientific explanation for eclipses as shadows cast by the Earth or the Moon, debunking mythological theories.

  • Sidereal Year: He calculated the length of the solar year to be 365.258 days, remarkably close to modern calculations.

  • Invention of Zero: While the concept existed, he was instrumental in defining its use in a decimal system.

  • Trigonometric Tables: He introduced the concept of Sine (Jya) and Versine (Utkrama-jya), which formed the basis of modern trigonometry.

  • Indeterminate Equations: He developed "Kuttaka," an algorithm for solving indeterminate equations (ax + by = c).

  • Spherical Earth: He asserted that the Earth is spherical (Gola) and provided a formula for its circumference.

  • Alphanumeric Notation: He used a unique system where consonants and vowels represented numbers, allowing for concise mathematical sutras.

  • Square and Cube Roots: He detailed the systematic steps to extract square roots and cube roots.

  • Motion of Planets: He calculated the orbits of planets based on an elliptical model rather than perfect circles.

  • Relative Motion: He explained relative motion using the famous analogy of a man in a boat seeing stationary objects on the shore move backward.

  • Area of a Triangle: He gave the formula: The area of a triangle is the product of the perpendicular and half the base.

  • Sum of Series: He provided formulas for the sum of the first n integers, their squares, and their cubes.

  • Planetary Diameters: He estimated the diameters of the Moon and Earth with high accuracy.

  • Epicycle Theory: He used a system of epicycles to explain the complex varying speeds of planets.

  • The Aryabhatta Satellite: In 1975, India’s first satellite was named after him to honor his contributions to space science.

  • Cosmology: He divided time into cycles (Yugas) and calculated their durations with mathematical precision.

  • No-Instrument Astronomy: Most of his observations were made without telescopes, relying on geometric logic and shadow lengths.
