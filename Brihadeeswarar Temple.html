Brihadeeswarar Temple
  • The Brihadisvara Temple (also known as the Thanjai Periya Kovil or Big Temple) is a 1,000-year-old Hindu temple in Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, dedicated to Lord Shiva

  • The temple architecture was designed in the period of chola dynasty which was also the pinnacle of chola art in 11th century

  • this 1,000-year-old temple has survived six earthquakes, showcasing advanced interlocking and weight-distribution techniques

  • the temple is masterpiece of ancient Indian engineering, built by Raja Raja Chola I Built entirely of interlocking granite stones.

  • the height of center temple tower is about 216 foot which make this temple one of the tallest temple and India and across the world

  • It showcases the ancient Indian engineering techniques and architectural knowledges .
  • At that time the technology was not much advanced but the ancient India was ahead of it's time
  • No-Mortar Interlocking Construction: The entire temple is built using a dry masonry method, where granite stones are interlocked together, requiring no binding agents like mortar or cement.

  • No Shadow Principle: The 216-foot vimana (tower) is designed so that its shadow does not fall on the ground at noon during the equinox, due to the clever cascading, interlocking placement of the stones.

  • 80-Ton Single Stone Capstone: The Kumbam (topmost structure) is carved from a single 80-ton granite block, lifted over 60 meters, likely using an inclined plane, according to historical accounts.

  • Monolithic Nandi Statue: The massive Nandi statue at the entrance is carved from a single piece of stone, measuring about 13 feet in length and 9 feet in height.

  • Granite Sourced from Far Away: There are no granite quarries within a 60-100 km radius of the temple, meaning over 130,000 tons of granite were transported from far-off places over 1,000 years ago.

  • Earthquake Resistance: Despite having no modern foundation, the interlocking, puzzle-like structure of the granite allows it to withstand severe natural disasters, including earthquakes

  • Musical Pillars: The temple has "musical pillars" in the main hall that produce different musical notes when struck.

  • Symmetrical and Axial Design: The entire complex follows strict, precise mathematical, and axial symmetry, a testament to advanced civil engineering in the 11th century

  • Intricate Carving Precision: Despite being made of hard granite, the temple features intricate, detailed sculptures and inscriptions, requiring advanced carving tools and techniques.

  • Rapid Construction: The massive, highly intricate temple was completed in just seven years, indicating a highly organized, advanced construction management system.

  • The architect of the Brihadeeswara Temple in Thanjavur was Kunjara Mallan Raja Raja Perunthachchan (also spelled Kunjara Mallan Raja Rama Perunthachan).

  • The temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, renowned for being built entirely of granite without cement or adhesive, relying on interlocking methods

  • Symbolic Abode (Dakshina Meru): The towering vimana (spire) was built to represent Mount Meru, the mythical home of Lord Shiva in Hindu cosmology. By naming it Dakshina Meru ("Meru of the South"), Rajaraja I symbolically offered Shiva a new home in southern India.

  • Commemoration of Conquests: Inscriptions suggest the temple celebrated Rajaraja I's military victories and his 29 years of successful rule.
  • The best time to visit the Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur is during the winter months from November to February. During this period, the weather is pleasant with temperatures ranging between 20°C and 30°C, making it comfortable for outdoor exploration and photography.

  • Temple Culture: Historically, the temple was center for Bharatanatyam, with inscriptions detailing the duties of hundreds of devadasis.
  • During the British period, specifically in the 18th to 19th centuries, the Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur came under British control. While they likely looted precious materials from the temple, the British did not destroy the site, recognizing its historical and architectural value.

  • Art History & Murals: Prof. S.K. Govindasami identified early Chola paintings, with further studies on Nayak-era paintings in the sanctum.
