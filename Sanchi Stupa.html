Sanchi Stupa
  • The Sanchi Stupa is located in the Raisen District of Madhya Pradesh 46 km north-east of Bhopal. Situated on a hilltop in Sanchi

  • The Great Stupa at Sanchi is one of the oldest stone structures in India, and an important monument to the historical architecture of India.

  • The best time to visit the Sanchi Stupa is during the winter months from October to March, when the weather is pleasant and ideal for exploring the open-air heritage site.The Sanchi Stupa is famous as one of India's oldest stone structures and a premier example of Buddhist art and architecture

  • The Sanchi Stupa was originally commissioned in the 3rd century BCE by the Mauryan Emperor Ashoka in Madhya Pradesh.

  • a premier example of Buddhist architecture, featuring a massive hemispherical brick dome (Anda) representing the cosmos.

  • It includes a central pillar (Yasti) for the triple umbrella (Chattra), symbolizing the three jewels of Buddhism, surrounded by a sacred railing (Vedika) and four elaborately carved stone gateways (Toranas)

  • reason to built sanchi stupa is to enshrine sacred relics of Lord Buddha and propagate Buddhism.After the Kalinga war, it served as a monument of peace, a pilgrimage site for devotees to honor Buddha, and a significant educational center.

  • The Buddhist monuments at Sanchi were collectively designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1989.The original Mauryan stupa was a simple hemispherical brick structure, about half the size of the current monument.

  • During the 2nd century BCE (Shunga period), the brick dome was encased in stone and doubled in size.

  • Ashoka chose Sanchi because it was the birthplace of his wife, Devi, and the location of their wedding.

  • The stupa sits on a hill approximately 91 meters high, about 46 kilometers northeast of Bhopal in the Raisen district of Madhya Pradesh.

  • The Sanchi Stupa is featured on the reverse side of the ₹200 denomination Indian banknote. Introduced by the Reserve Bank of India in August 2017.

  • Despite being a primary Buddhist pilgrimage site, there is no evidence that Gautama Buddha ever visited Sanchi during his lifetime.

  • the famous toranas (gateways) are made of stone, an inscription on the Southern Gateway reveals they were actually carved by the Guild of Ivory Carvers from Vidisha.

  • This explains the extreme, "jewelry-like" precision and detail that mimics wood or ivory carving.After being abandoned in the 13th century, Sanchi was forgotten for nearly 600 years. The dense jungle that grew over it acted as a natural shield, protecting it from destruction .

  • When the site was "rediscovered" in 1818 by British General Henry Taylor, it fell victim to amateur archaeologists and treasure hunters who caused significant damage by breaching the dome in search of relics.

  • In the 19th century, the French and the British both wanted to take the ornate gateways to Europe.

  • The Nawab of Bhopal even offered the Southern Gateway as a gift to Queen Victoria, but only plaster casts were ultimately sent, keeping the original structures in India.

  • In the early sculptures (1st century BCE), Buddha is never depicted in human form.

  • Instead, he is represented through symbols like a riderless horse, an empty throne, footprints, or the Dharma wheel.

  • the Great Stupa houses Buddha's relics, Stupa No. 3 contains the remains of his two chief disciples, Sariputra and Mahamoggallana.
